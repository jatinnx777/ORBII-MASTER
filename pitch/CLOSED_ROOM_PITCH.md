# ORBII — Closed-Room University Pitch

For a small room, no slides, no projector. You, sitting across a table from a
Dean / Registrar / E-Cell head. This is a conversation you steer, not a
performance. They will interrupt. That is good. Let them.

Carry ONE printed page: the numbers sheet at the bottom. That is the only prop
you need. Slide it across when you get to the money.

Talk time is about 5 minutes if uninterrupted, but plan for it to become a
15-minute back-and-forth. That is a good meeting, not a derailed one.

**[YOUR ...]** = things only you can fill, and they must be true.

---

## How to hold the room (read this once, then forget it)

- Sit up, hands still, speak slower than feels natural. Confidence in a small room
  is calm, not volume.
- Look at whoever has the power to say yes, usually the senior-most person, but
  bring the others in with your eyes.
- When they interrupt with a question, that is buying interest. Answer it, then
  say "and that connects to the ask, which is..." and steer back.
- You are not begging. You are offering them a safer campus, a startup with their
  name on it, and money back. Sit like it.

---

## 1. The open (seated, direct)

[No theatrics. In a small room, a gimmick feels forced. Open with a quiet, true
sentence and let it land. Look at the senior person.]

> "Thank you for the time. I'll be direct, because your time is short and I've
> built something real.
>
> Every safety app a student on this campus has right now shares one flaw: it needs
> her to reach her phone, unlock it, find a button, hold it. In the actual moment
> she's in danger, hands full, phone in her bag, running, that's the one thing she
> can't do.
>
> I built ORBII to fix that. She just says the word 'help,' out loud, and her phone
> does the rest. No button. And I built the whole thing here, as your student."

[That is your entire hook in four sentences. "Built here, as your student" is the
line that makes this their project, not a vendor's. Pause after it.]

---

## 2. What it does (keep it to 30 seconds, concrete)

> "Here's the whole thing. She says 'help.' The phone hears it on the device
> itself, the audio never leaves the phone, never touches a server, never gets
> sold. A ten-second countdown starts, so a false alarm is one tap to cancel. If
> she doesn't cancel, her chosen circle gets her live location, every ORBII user
> nearby gets a full-screen alert, and the phone starts recording. She never
> touched the screen.
>
> And it works even with no internet, the alert hops phone to phone over Bluetooth.
> A campus is the perfect place for that, hundreds of phones in range of each other
> all day."

[If they look skeptical on the offline part, be honest: "that piece is built and
I'm testing it on real phones now." Never oversell it. Move on.]

---

## 3. Why I'm sitting here and not just on the Play Store

> "I could put this on the Play Store tomorrow. I'm sitting here instead because a
> campus is where this matters most, and because you get three things from it that
> no outside vendor can offer you.
>
> One, safety. Every student protected, hands-free, the day you say yes. That's the
> answer to every worried parent and every safety review.
>
> Two, the name. This was built here, by your student, out of your E-Cell. When it
> grows, the story is this college's. That moves reputation and admissions, and you
> can't buy it.
>
> Three, and this is the part people don't expect, it costs the college nothing.
> Let me show you how."

[Now reach for the one sheet and slide it across. This is the moment the meeting
turns from "nice project" to "business proposal."]

---

## 4. The model (slide the sheet across as you say this)

> "The university includes ORBII for every student, the same way you already bundle
> insurance into the fee. Two hundred rupees per student, for the whole year.
> That's under seventeen rupees a month. Less than one samosa, for a year of safety.
>
> This isn't a strange idea, it's the standard one. You already pay per student for
> insurance, for the ERP, for antivirus, for the learning platform. Safety is the
> one thing missing from that list.
>
> And because it's bundled into the student fee, exactly like insurance already is,
> the college isn't out of pocket a single rupee. Every student is protected, and it
> costs the institution nothing."

[Let them read the sheet. Stay quiet for a beat. Then point at the row for your
campus size:]

> "For our roughly **[YOUR CAMPUS SIZE]** students, that's **[₹ from the sheet]** a
> year, all of it bundled into the fee, so the college pays nothing out of pocket and
> every student is covered."

## 5. The four asks (say them as a numbered list, out loud)

[Do not soften these or bury them. An administration wants to know exactly what it
is approving. Count them on your fingers if it helps.]

> "So here's what I'm asking the university for. Four things.
>
> One, back the launch with two and a half lakh rupees. That takes ORBII from a
> working app to a registered, legal, launched company. The breakdown's on the
> sheet, and the biggest line isn't marketing, it's proving that when the alarm goes
> out, a real person comes.
>
> Two, attendance relaxation. I can't build a company and hold perfect attendance in
> the same day. The Ministry of Education's own startup policy encourages colleges
> to grant founders exactly this. I'm asking you to formally allow it, so I'm not
> choosing between my degree and the thing you're backing.
>
> Three, a small workspace on campus, a room or a few desks in the incubation centre
> or E-Cell, where my team and I can work and test. It keeps your flagship startup
> here.
>
> Four, adopt ORBII for every student. Be the first campus in India where every
> single student is protected, hands-free."

---

## 6. Why me (the one part you must make true)

> "[YOUR WHY — the real reason you started this, in one honest sentence.]
>
> I'm not a team of forty. I built this myself, the listening engine, the network,
> the app, the offline part, all of it, backed by [YOUR BACKING], on a budget most
> people would call impossible. If that's what one student could build here with
> almost nothing, imagine what it becomes with the university behind it."

---

## 7. The close (quiet, then stop talking)

> "You have a choice most colleges never get. You can wait for something to go
> wrong and then form a committee. Or you can be the campus that was already ahead,
> where every student was protected, and where the startup that did it was born.
>
> I'd rather it be born here, with you.
>
> That's my ask. I'm happy to take any question you have."

[Then genuinely stop. Silence is fine. Let them respond. Do not fill it by
over-explaining.]

## Q&A ammo (the questions you WILL get in that room)

- **"Isn't ₹200 per student a lot / childish?"** "It's the standard model. You
  already pay per student for insurance, the ERP, antivirus, the LMS. Safety is the
  one missing from that list. ₹200 for a year is less than a movie ticket."
- **"Will students and parents accept the fee?"** "It's ₹200 for the whole year,
  bundled, not a separate charge. Parents pay far more for far less every semester,
  and this buys the one thing they worry about most."
- **"What's the college's liability if something fails?"** "ORBII lowers your
  exposure, it doesn't raise it. Audio is processed on the phone and never uploaded,
  so there's no data risk to the college under the DPDP Act, and the company, not
  the college, carries product liability. Every student having a working safety tool
  is exactly what a safety audit wants to see."
- **"Why fund you AND pay per student?"** "The ₹2.5L is one-time, to reach launch
  before revenue exists. The per-student model is what keeps ORBII alive afterwards,
  and it's bundled into the fee, so it never costs the college out of pocket. You're
  backing a safety tool for every student and a startup born on your campus."
- **"Does the offline Bluetooth thing actually work?"** "It's built and I'm testing
  it on real phones now. A dense campus is the ideal place to prove it. I won't call
  it battle-proven before it is."
- **"How is this different from [existing app]?"** "Every one of them needs her to
  reach and press her phone. ORBII is hands-free, on-device so nothing's sold, and
  works with no internet. Those three together, nobody else has."

## Notes for you (not part of the pitch)

- **Fill your real campus size and the matching rupee figures before you walk in.**
  Point at the exact row on the sheet. Real numbers close; round guesses don't.
- **Check your college's attendance-relaxation policy first,** so ask two becomes
  "approve what your own policy allows," not "invent something new."
- **Don't try to live-demo the offline mesh.** It's untested on two phones and can't
  be shown on one. If you demo anything, demo hands-free Voice SOS on your own phone,
  and only if the room asks. A phone that fails live is worse than no demo.
- **Never inflate a number or your backing.** They can check, and on a safety
  product one caught exaggeration costs you all of it.
- **If you forget everything, remember the four asks and the one sheet.** ₹2.5L,
  attendance, workspace, adopt for every student. Everything else is conversation.
- **File a provisional patent early** (before you show the idea widely). In India a
  provisional patent is cheap and buys you 12 months to lock a priority date on the
  hands-free, on-device, offline-mesh combination. It's in the funds sheet under IP
  protection. Protecting the idea before it spreads is worth doing first.
