# ORBII — University Partnership Pitch

A spoken script for pitching the UNIVERSITY (not investors). Read it out loud.
`[notes in brackets]` are delivery cues, not lines to say. Run time about 6 to 7
minutes; a 90-second version is at the bottom.

This is a partnership pitch, not a donation request. You are not a student asking
for pocket money. You are offering the university three things it actually wants:
safer students, a flagship startup born on their campus, and money flowing back
into campus life. You are asking for four things in return. Deliver it that way.

The parts only you can fill are marked **[YOUR ...]**. They must be true and
yours. Do not fake a number in front of an administration that can check.

---

## 0. Before you speak

[This room is a Dean, a Registrar, maybe the E-Cell head. They do not think like
investors. They think about student safety, the college's name, liability, and
budgets. Every line below is aimed at one of those four. Walk to the centre. Let
it settle. Begin calm and low.]

---

## 1. The open — the word

> "Say the word 'help' out loud. Right now. Quietly is fine."

[Wait. Actually wait. Then:]

> "Nothing happened.
>
> Your phone heard you. It is the closest thing to every student on this campus.
> And when a person says the one word a human says in danger, it does nothing.
>
> I'm Jatin. I'm a student here. And I've spent the last year building the thing
> that answers, on this campus, with almost nothing."

[Name, that you are THEIR student, and a mission, in one breath. The fact that
this was built here is your single biggest lever with a university. Use it early.]

---

## 2. The problem is yours too — safety is the university's responsibility

> "Here is the part that matters to you specifically.
>
> Every parent who sends their daughter here hands you a responsibility. Every
> late lab, every walk back to the hostel, every auto ride off campus at night,
> that is on the university's conscience, and increasingly on the university's
> record.
>
> And the safety apps that exist all share one fatal flaw: they need her to reach
> her phone, open it, find a button, hold it. In the actual moment, being grabbed,
> running, phone in her bag, that is exactly what she cannot do.
>
> We didn't build a better button. We got rid of the button."

[Land "we got rid of the button" slowly. Then connect it straight back to them:
this is the university's duty of care, and you are handing them a way to meet it.]

---

## 3. What ORBII is — 30 seconds

> "ORBII listens for the word 'help.' On the phone itself. The audio never leaves
> the device, never touches a server, never gets sold, because it never goes
> anywhere. She says 'help,' and a ten-second countdown starts so a false alarm is
> one tap to cancel. If she doesn't cancel, her circle gets her live location,
> every ORBII user nearby gets a full-screen alert, and her phone starts recording
> evidence. She never touched the screen.
>
> And here is the part built for India: it works even with no internet. The alert
> hops phone to phone over Bluetooth, and goes out as a plain SMS at the same time.
> A campus is the perfect place for that, hundreds of ORBII phones in Bluetooth
> range of each other, all the time."

[The Bluetooth line is honest: say it is built and in testing. But a dense campus
is genuinely the ideal case for a phone-to-phone mesh, so make that point.]

---

## 4. Why the UNIVERSITY should care — three reasons in their language

> "So why am I standing in front of the university and not just putting this on the
> Play Store? Because a campus is where this matters most, and because you get
> three things out of it that no vendor can sell you.
>
> One: safety. Every student, protected, hands-free, the day you say yes. That is
> the strongest answer you can give a worried parent or a safety audit.
>
> Two: the story. ORBII was built here, by your own student, out of your own
> E-Cell. When it grows, and it will, the headline is this college's name. That is
> the kind of thing that moves rankings and recruitment, and you cannot buy it.
>
> Three: it costs the college nothing. I will come to the numbers, but the short
> version is the fee is bundled into what students already pay, so the institution is
> not out of pocket a single rupee, and every student is protected."

[These three, safety, reputation, zero-cost protection, are the entire pitch to an
administration. Everything else is detail. Say them clearly and pause on each.]

---

## 5. The model — how this actually makes money (say it with confidence)

[This is the heart of the new pitch. Do not apologise for it. Per-student pricing
is exactly how safety and software are sold to institutions, insurance, ERP,
antivirus, the LMS you already pay for, all priced per student. You are proposing
the same, standard model.]

> "Here is how ORBII becomes a real business, and how the college wins with me.
>
> The university includes ORBII for every student, the way you already bundle
> insurance and other services into the fee. The price is two hundred rupees per
> student, for the year. That is under seventeen rupees a month. Less than one
> samosa, for a full year of hands-free safety.
>
> This is not a new idea. Universities already pay per student for insurance, for
> the ERP, for anti-virus, for the learning platform. Safety belongs on that list,
> and right now it is the one missing.
>
> And here is what makes it easy to say yes: because it is bundled into the student
> fee, exactly like insurance already is, the college pays nothing out of pocket.
> Every student is protected, at zero cost to the institution."

[The zero-cost line is your close. It removes the only real objection an
administration has, budget. Deliver it slowly.]

---

## 5b. The math — show it, don't hand-wave it

[Have this ready on one slide or one sheet. Fill in your real campus size. Numbers
below are illustrative; use YOUR count.]

> "Let me put real numbers on it. Take **[YOUR CAMPUS SIZE, e.g. 5,000]** students.
>
> At two hundred rupees each, that is **[e.g. ten lakh rupees]** a year for ORBII to
> build the network out. And it is not the college paying it, it is bundled into the
> student fee, the same way insurance already is.
>
> So the college spends nothing out of pocket, and every student is protected. That
> is the deal."

[If they push on whether students will accept a fee: it is ₹200 for the year, less
than a movie ticket, for their own safety, and it is bundled, not a separate ask.
Say it in one breath and move on.]

---

## 6. What I'm asking for — the four asks, plainly

[State all four clearly. Do not bury them. An administration wants to know exactly
what it is being asked to approve.]

> "So here is what I am asking this university for.
>
> **One. Backing to launch: two and a half lakh rupees.** This gets ORBII from a
> working app to a registered, legal, launched company. I have the full breakdown,
> and the single biggest line is not marketing, it is proving that when the alarm
> goes out, a real person comes. I would rather prove that than assume it.
>
> **Two. Attendance relaxation.** I cannot build a company and keep perfect
> attendance in the same twenty-four hours. The Ministry of Education's own
> innovation and startup policy encourages colleges to give student founders
> exactly this. I am asking this college to formally grant it, so I am not forced
> to choose between my degree and the thing you are backing.
>
> **Three. A workspace on campus.** A small dedicated space, a room or a few desks
> in the incubation centre or E-Cell, where I and my team can actually work, meet,
> and test. It keeps ORBII on campus, which is where you want your flagship startup
> to stay.
>
> **Four. Adopt ORBII for every student.** The model I just described. Be the first
> campus in the country where every single student is protected hands-free."

[Say the four as a numbered list, out loud, "one, two, three, four." It signals
you have thought this through like a founder, not a dreamer.]

---

## 7. Why me, why now

[Here is where **[YOUR WHY]** goes. The single most important 20 seconds. The real
reason a student built this and did not stop. One true sentence beats a paragraph.]

> "[YOUR WHY — the real reason you started, in your own words.]
>
> I am not a team of forty. I built this myself, the listening engine, the network,
> the app, the offline mesh, all of it, backed by [YOUR BACKING — e.g. this college's
> E-Cell], on a budget most people would call impossible.
>
> If this is what one student could build here with almost nothing, imagine what it
> becomes with the university behind it."

[**[YOUR BACKING]**: only what is true and already real. They can check.]

---

## 8. The close

> "You have a choice most colleges never get. You can be the campus that waited for
> a tragedy and then formed a committee. Or you can be the campus that was first,
> the one where every student was already protected, and where the startup that did
> it was born.
>
> The first time I said 'help' to a phone and it actually answered, I couldn't
> un-know it. I want every student here to have that.
>
> Say the word. ORBII answers."

[Let it sit two seconds. Then, normally:]

> "Thank you. I'd love your questions."

## Delivery cheat sheet

**The one-liner:**
> "ORBII is the hands-free safety app built on this campus: a student says 'help'
> and her phone alerts her circle and everyone nearby, working even with no
> internet. I want the university to protect every student with it, at ₹200 a
> student a year, bundled into the fee so it costs the college nothing."

**The four things they must remember when you leave the room:**
1. Built here, by their student. The story is the college's.
2. It protects every student hands-free, even with no internet.
3. ₹200 per student, bundled like insurance. Costs the college nothing out of pocket.
4. Four asks: ₹2.5L to launch, attendance relaxation, a workspace, campus-wide adoption.

## Appendix C — Q&A ammo (say only if asked)

- **"Isn't per-student a bit much / childish?"** "It's the standard institutional
  model. You already pay per student for insurance, the ERP, antivirus, the LMS.
  Safety is the one missing from that list. ₹200 a year is less than a movie ticket
  for hands-free protection. This is how B2B safety is sold everywhere."
- **"Will students / parents accept the fee?"** "It's ₹200 for the whole year,
  bundled, not a separate charge. Parents pay far more for far less every semester.
  And it buys the one thing they worry about most: their child's safety."
- **"What's our liability if it fails?"** "ORBII reduces your exposure, it doesn't
  add to it. The audio is processed on-device and never uploaded, so there is no
  data-privacy risk to the college under the DPDP Act. And every student having a
  working safety tool is exactly what a safety audit wants to see. The company, not
  the college, carries product liability."
- **"Why should we fund you and also pay per student?"** "The ₹2.5L is one-time,
  to get to launch before revenue exists. The per-student model is what makes ORBII
  sustainable afterwards, and it's bundled into the fee, so it never costs the
  college out of pocket. You are seeding a safety tool for every student and a
  company born on your campus."
- **"What about the offline / Bluetooth features?"** "Built and in testing on real
  phones now. A dense campus is the ideal place to prove them, hundreds of phones
  in range at all times. I'll never tell you it's battle-proven before it is."

## Notes for you (not part of the pitch)

- **Fill your real campus size before you present.** The math in Appendix B is the
  most persuasive page for an administration, but only if the number is real.
- **Verify your college's attendance-relaxation policy in advance.** Many colleges,
  under the Ministry of Education's Student Innovation and Startup Policy, already
  allow it. Walk in knowing whether yours does, so ask two is "approve what your own
  policy allows," not "invent something new."
- **Never fake a user count or an inflated backing.** An administration can and will
  check. Your honesty is part of why they should trust a safety product with their
  students.
- **Don't live-demo the offline Bluetooth mesh.** It's untested on two phones and
  you can't demo a mesh with one anyway. Demo the proven thing: hands-free Voice
  SOS. Have a 20-second screen recording as a backup.
- **The four asks are the spine.** If you forget everything else, walk out having
  clearly stated: ₹2.5L, attendance, workspace, per-student adoption.
