# ORBII — The Pitch

A spoken script, not a document. Read it out loud. The `[notes in brackets]` are
delivery cues, not lines to say. Total run time about 5 to 6 minutes; cut-down
versions are at the bottom.

Two things only you can fill in are marked **[YOUR ...]**. Do not let me write
those for you. They have to be true and they have to be yours.

---

## 0. Before you speak

[Walk to the center. Don't rush to the slides. Let the room settle. Your first
weapon is silence, not a sentence. Look at one person. Then begin, calm and low.]

---

## 1. The open — the word

> "Say the word 'help' out loud. Right now. Quietly is fine."

[Wait. Actually wait. Some of the room will mutter it, some won't. Either way you
win. Then:]

> "Nothing happened.
>
> Your phone heard you. It's the closest thing to you in this entire room. It's
> closer to you than the person sitting next to you. And when you said the one
> word a human says when they're in danger, it did nothing.
>
> I'm Jatin. I'm nineteen. And I've spent the last year building the thing that
> answers."

[That's your intro. Name, age, and a mission, in three seconds, earned instead of
announced. Do NOT open with "Hi, I'm Jatin, founder and CEO of ORBII, a women's
safety app." That sentence tells them nothing and sounds like everyone else.]

---

## 2. The real problem — reframe

> "Here's what nobody says about women's safety apps. There are hundreds of them.
> She's probably got one right now.
>
> And every single one has the same fatal flaw: it needs her to reach her phone.
> Open it. Find the button. Press it. Hold it three seconds.
>
> But think about the actual moment. Her hands aren't free. She's being grabbed,
> she's running, the phone is in her bag, her fingers are shaking. The one moment
> the app was built for is the exact moment she can't use it.
>
> We didn't build a better button. We got rid of the button."

[This is the whole thesis. Land it slowly. "We got rid of the button" is a line
you pause after.]

---

## 3. The betrayal — why trust is the real market

> "There's a second problem, and it's uglier.
>
> The most popular family safety app in the world got caught selling the precise
> location of its users, including children, to data brokers. It got sued. Teenagers
> made videos begging each other to delete it, because being 'kept safe' had turned
> into being watched and sold.
>
> So the market isn't just 'an app that works.' It's an app a woman can actually
> trust with the most sensitive thing she owns: where she is."

[You're setting up your two unfair advantages: it works hands-free, and it's
trustworthy. Now you deliver both.]

---

## 4. The turn — what ORBII is

> "So we asked one question. What if the phone just... listened. And what if it
> never sent a single second of that anywhere.
>
> ORBII listens for the word. On the phone itself. The audio never leaves the
> device, never touches a server, never gets sold, because it never goes anywhere.
> She says 'help,' and her phone, which was useless a second ago, becomes the
> fastest way to get help she has."

[Beat. This is the reveal. Let "it never leaves the device" hang in the air,
because it's the answer to the betrayal you just described.]

---

## 5. How it actually works — 30 seconds, concrete, no cliché

> "Here's the whole thing, start to finish.
>
> She says the word. ORBII hears it, even with the screen off, even in her pocket,
> even in a noisy street, because we tuned it for exactly that.
>
> A ten-second countdown starts, so a false alarm is one tap to cancel. Nobody
> gets a fake SOS.
>
> If she doesn't cancel, three things fire at once. Her circle, the people she
> chose, get her live location. Every ORBII user nearby gets a full-screen alert
> that someone close needs help. And her phone quietly starts recording evidence.
>
> She never touched the screen. That's the product."

[Say this like you're describing something obvious and inevitable, because you
want them thinking "why doesn't this already exist." Speed up slightly here, it's
the one place energy should rise.]

---

## 6. The line nobody else can draw — consent

> "And the whole time, she is in control of who sees her. ORBII shows her, always,
> exactly who can see her location, and lets her change it in one tap.
>
> Life360 hides the watching. We put it on the screen. That's not a feature. That's
> the entire difference between safety and surveillance."

---

## 7. The model — free for her, paid where it costs

[This is where you protect your money and sound like a real business, not a
charity. Deliver it confidently, no apology.]

> "ORBII is free for every woman. Alert your circle, reach nearby ORBII members,
> call 112 in one tap. A safety baseline should never be behind a paywall, and it
> isn't.
>
> ORBII Plus, ninety-nine rupees a month, adds our verified responder network:
> background-checked people who are dispatched to reach you and are accountable for
> it. Think of it like roadside assistance. Anyone can flag down a passerby. Members
> get a professional who is guaranteed to come.
>
> The free tier is how we protect every woman. The paid tier is how we pay the
> people who show up. One funds the other."

[If asked about scams/abuse: verified responders are paid, so they're only ever
dispatched for paying members, which means fake SOS can't farm payouts. Firing is
rate-limited and the countdown blocks accidents. Say it in one breath and move on.]

---

## 7b. Who the helpers are — say this before they ask

[This is the number-one question you will get: "who actually comes?" Answer it on
your own terms before they can make it sound like a weakness. Deliver it plainly,
like you've already solved it, because you have.]

> "You're wondering who actually shows up. Not random heroes, that model is a
> fantasy and it fails.
>
> Our responders are people already on the street and already motivated: gig
> workers like auto and delivery riders who know the area, and, where we start,
> trained campus responders. Vetted, rated, and paid per response.
>
> And their job is not to fight anyone. It's to reach you fast, be a witness, make
> noise, and get the police there. Presence, not combat. That's what keeps them
> safe, keeps us legal, and keeps them saying yes."

[The three words that make this bulletproof: "presence, not combat." If they push
on liability, that phrase is your whole defense.]

---

## 8. Why me, why now

[Here is where **[YOUR WHY]** goes. The single most important 20 seconds of the
pitch and the only part I refuse to script, because a fabricated reason is worse
than none. Tell them the true thing that made a nineteen-year-old build this and
not stop. One real sentence beats a paragraph of drama.]

> "[YOUR WHY — the real reason you started, in your own words.]
>
> I'm not a team of forty. I built this myself. The listening engine, the network,
> the app in your hand, all of it. And I did it backed by [YOUR BACKING — e.g. my
> college E-Cell and the Haryana government], on a budget most people would call
> impossible.
>
> I'm telling you that not as an excuse, but because it's the proof: if this is
> what one nineteen-year-old could build with almost nothing, imagine what this
> becomes with you behind it."

[**[YOUR BACKING]**: state only what's true and already real. Don't inflate it.
Real and small beats big and fake, and any investor will check.]

---

## 9. The vision — bigger than an app

> "I'm not building a safety app. Safety apps are a graveyard.
>
> I'm building the layer that changes what a phone does when the person holding it
> can't. Today it's 'help.' Tomorrow it's a fall, a crash, a child's voice, a word
> in any language. The phone stops being a thing she has to reach for and becomes
> the thing that reaches for her.
>
> Every woman in India first. Then everyone who's ever been alone and afraid. That's
> the whole market, and it's the whole point."

---

## 10. The ask + close

[State the ask plainly. Fill in the real number and what it buys. Investors trust
specificity, not vibes.]

> "I'm raising [YOUR ASK] to [the one or two things it unlocks: reach, responder
> network in the first cities, and getting this into hands before the next
> semester]."

[Then stop selling. Land the plane on the word you opened with. Slow all the way
down.]

> "The first time I said 'help' to a phone, and it actually answered, I knew I
> couldn't un-know it.
>
> I want every woman in this country to have that.
>
> Say the word. ORBII answers."

[Don't say "thank you" into that. Let it sit for two seconds. Then, normally:]

> "Thank you. I'd love your questions."

## Delivery cheat sheet

**The one-liner (elevator, 1 sentence):**
> "ORBII is the safety app that gets rid of the button. She says 'help,' and her
> phone, listening on-device so nothing is ever sold, alerts her circle and the
> people nearest her, hands-free."

**The 30-second version (if you only get one breath):**
> "Every women's safety app has the same flaw: it needs her to reach her phone in
> the one moment she can't. ORBII gets rid of the button. It listens for the word
> 'help,' entirely on the device so her audio is never uploaded or sold, and the
> second she says it, her circle and nearby ORBII members get her live location
> hands-free. Free for every woman; a paid verified-responder network on top. I'm
> nineteen, I built the whole thing, and I want it in every hand in India."

**Three things they must remember when you sit down:**
1. ORBII got rid of the button. It listens.
2. On-device. Nothing is sold. The opposite of Life360.
3. Free safety for everyone, paid guaranteed response on top.

## Appendix B — Company & legal (Q&A ammo)

- **Structure:** Private Limited company (I have a co-founder, so we meet the two-
  director requirement). It shields us personally from liability, which matters for
  a safety product, and it's the clean base to raise real money later. Roughly
  ₹10-20k all-in through a CA, about two to three weeks via SPICe+.
- **Why not cheaper options:** a sole proprietorship gives zero liability
  protection (dangerous for an app people trust with their safety); an LLP is
  cheaper but investors prefer Pvt Ltd.
- **Data law:** because we handle location and audio, the DPDP Act 2023 applies.
  Our posture already fits it: audio is processed on-device and never uploaded, and
  the user always sees and controls who can see her. The legal spend gets the
  privacy policy and terms lawyer-reviewed so we're covered.
- **The one-liner if they probe compliance:** "We built privacy-first on purpose.
  The audio never leaves the phone, and we never sell location. That's not just
  ethics, it's our legal moat and the opposite of why people distrust Life360."

## Notes for you (not part of the pitch)

- **Pacing beats content.** This script has more than most slots allow. Practice
  the full version, then cut to fit. The open (section 1), the reframe (section 2),
  and the close (section 10) are load-bearing. Everything else is trimmable.
- **The interactive open is a risk worth taking.** If the room is stiff and nobody
  says "help," you still win: "Nothing happened" works even better against silence.
- **Never fake a number or a user count.** On a safety product, a judge who catches
  one inflated stat stops believing all of them. Your honesty is part of the pitch.
- **Slides, if any, should be almost empty.** One word per slide. The word "help."
  A map. A price. You are the pitch, not the deck.
