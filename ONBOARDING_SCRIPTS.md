# ORBII onboarding, video scripts

Nine clips. Two languages. Roughly three and a half minutes of footage total.

Read the rules before the scripts. They are why the scripts are written the way
they are, and getting one wrong costs a reshoot.

---

## The rules

**No fear.** No dark streets, no statistics, no "1 in 3 women". She already lives
with it. Selling it back to her is manipulative, it makes the app feel like an
accusation, and it is why most safety marketing feels awful to the people it is
aimed at.

**Show, do not sell.** The strongest thing ORBII has is that it works without
being touched. Say the word on camera and let the phone do it. One demonstration
beats every adjective.

**Second person, present tense.** "You say a word." Not "users can trigger".

**Never claim what is not proven.** The offline mesh is not live. Do not mention
it in any clip. Where a limit exists, say it. That is the point of clip 9.

**Ten seconds is about 25 words.** Thirty is about 75. Count them. A script that
runs long gets rushed on camera and rushed sounds untrustworthy.

**Hinglish is correct.** Say "screen lock", "internet", "location" in English
inside a Hindi sentence, because that is how people actually speak. Formal
Hindi here sounds like a government notice.

**Same person in every clip.** Onboarding is one conversation. A woman, in her
twenties, on a phone camera, in a normal room. Not a studio, not a presenter
voice. The person she is meeting should look like someone she knows.

## 1. Language

**No video.** This screen is instant, two buttons, before anything loads. A
person who cannot read English should never see an English sentence first.

## 2. What ORBII is · 30 seconds

The one that decides whether she finishes. Everything else is detail.

**English**

> Most safety apps need you to find your phone, unlock it, open the app, and
> press something.
>
> ORBII doesn't.
>
> You say one word out loud. Help. Bachao. Madad.
>
> And the phone acts. Screen locked, phone in your bag, no internet needed.
>
> Your people get your live location. People nearby are told. 112 stays one tap
> away.
>
> That's the whole idea. Let's set it up, it takes two minutes.

**Hindi**

> ज़्यादातर safety apps में पहले फ़ोन ढूँढो, unlock करो, app खोलो, फिर बटन दबाओ।
>
> ORBII में ऐसा नहीं है।
>
> आप बस एक शब्द बोलिए। Help. बचाओ. मदद.
>
> और फ़ोन खुद काम करेगा। Screen lock हो, फ़ोन बैग में हो, internet ना हो, फिर भी।
>
> आपके लोगों को live location मिल जाएगी। आस-पास वालों को पता चल जाएगा। 112 एक tap दूर।
>
> बस इतनी सी बात है। चलिए setup करते हैं, दो मिनट लगेंगे।

**Direction.** She says "help" out loud mid-clip and the phone on the table
lights up. Do not cut away. That single unbroken shot is the most persuasive
three seconds in the whole app.

**Hindi**

## 7. Battery settings · 10 seconds

The least glamorous clip and the one that decides whether ORBII works in six
months. Xiaomi, Realme, Oppo and Vivo close background apps aggressively.

**English**

> Your phone shuts down background apps to save battery. If it closes ORBII, it
> can't hear you.
>
> This one screen stops that. It's the most important thing here.

**Hindi**

> Battery बचाने के लिए फ़ोन background apps बंद कर देता है। ORBII बंद हो गया तो
> वो आपकी आवाज़ नहीं सुन पाएगा।
>
> यह एक screen उसे रोक देती है। यहाँ की सबसे ज़रूरी चीज़ यही है।

## 9. If things fail · 30 seconds

The differentiator. **Nobody else in this category says any of this on camera**,
and it will buy more trust than every other clip put together.

**English**

> Here's what we won't pretend.
>
> If your phone is off, nothing works. Not ORBII, not any app. Anyone who tells
> you otherwise is selling you something.
>
> If you have signal but no internet, ORBII puts the message in front of you,
> already written, ready to send in one tap.
>
> If you have nothing at all, your alarm is saved and goes out the second you're
> connected again.
>
> And no app brings the police faster than 112 does. That button is always
> there, and sometimes it's the right one.
>
> We'd rather you knew.

**Hindi**

> जो हम नहीं कहेंगे, पहले वो सुन लीजिए।
>
> फ़ोन बंद है तो कुछ काम नहीं करेगा। ना ORBII, ना कोई और app. जो कहे कि करेगा, वो
> आपको कुछ बेच रहा है।
>
> Signal है पर internet नहीं, तो ORBII message तैयार करके सामने रख देगा, एक tap
> में चला जाएगा।
>
> कुछ भी नहीं है, तो आपका alarm save हो जाएगा और connection आते ही चला जाएगा।
>
> और police को 112 से तेज़ कोई app नहीं बुला सकता। वो बटन हमेशा है, और कभी-कभी
> वही सही है।
>
> हमें लगा आपको पता होना चाहिए।

**Do not soften this clip.** Every sentence in it is true today, and its whole
value is that it is the one nobody else would record.

## Production notes

**Portrait, 1080x1920.** Shot on a phone is genuinely better here than a studio.

**Face in the top two thirds.** The question sheet covers the bottom third of
the screen. Anything framed low is invisible.

**Record the demo clips in one take.** Clips 2 and 6 have the phone actually
firing. A cut in the middle reads as a fake, and this is the exact claim people
are sceptical about.

**Room tone, not silence.** Phone mic in a normal room. Studio-clean audio makes
it feel like an ad.

**Film 9 first.** If the honesty clip works, the tone for the other eight
follows. If it feels preachy, the whole set needs rewriting, and better to find
that out on the first day.

**Hindi first.** Most of your first thousand users will pick it, and if the
English is shot first the Hindi ends up as a translation rather than a script.
